# Generalized Biot's theory and Mandel's problem of multiple-porosity and multiplepermeability poroelasticity

Amin Mehrabian1,² and Younane N.Abousleiman³

1Nowat Halliurton EnergyServices,Baroid,TechnologyCenter,Houston,Texas,USA,²Mewbourne SchoolofPetroleum andGeological Engineering,PoroMechanics Institute,UniversityofOklahoma,Norman,Oklahoma,USAMewbourne Schoolof PetroleumandGeologicalEngineering,ShoolofCivilEngineeringandEnvironmentalScience,Conocohilips School of Geologyand Geophysics,PoroMechanics Institute,Universityof Oklahoma,Norman,Oklahoma,USA

Abstract This paper finds in Biot's theory of poroelasticity a complete and consistent extension to the general case of multiple-porosityand multiple-permeability,fluid-saturated,and linearly elastic media.The constitutive stress-strain relationsfora medium identified with this extensionare presented,andthecoefficient matrix of mechanical properties associated with these relations is derived from the corresponding intrinsic properties of its single-porosity constituents.The closed form analytical solution to Mandel's problem is upgraded to the case being considered in this study.This problem addresses the transient consolidation of a porous elastic slab of rectangular geometry, when confined from the top and botom.A numerical example solution for shale with laboratory setup of Mandel's problem is provided. Resultsare comparedfor the cases of single-,double-,and triple-porosity solutions.

# 1. Introduction

Sincethe adventof basic ideas in the theory offluid seepage infissured porous media [Barenblatt etal.,1960; Warren and Root,1963], much effort has been made to foster the dual-continuum method in mechanical modeling of materials with more than one distinguishable porous structures.The dual-diffusivity theory of Aifantis [1979,1980a,1980b] represents earlier investigations on the subject, with a focus on the species difusion through arigid solid matrix.More recently,adiscrete representation of the theoryhas been published by Kalampakas and Aifantis [2012]by use of the graph theory.It has been shown that phenomenological coefficients of dual-difusivity formulation at this level of investigation require some extra constraints, depending on geometry and graph topology,which cannot be predicted bya macroscopic approach.Another recent and interesting advancement on this subject is the gradient nanomechanics [Aifantis,2011],which considers interactions between grain boundaries and the bulk phase.These interactions are presented in the form of extra gradient terms contributing to the conservation laws of the associated constitutive variables which govern the nanoscale behavior of the material.

Alternatively,the theory of dual-porosity and dual-permeability poroelasticity [Aifantis,1986; Wilson and Aifantis,1982; Beskos andAifantis,1986; Berrymanand Wang,1995]stems from Biot's theoryof poroelasticity [Biot,1941,1955] to address the coupled fluid flow and deformation processes of such media. Early on, Lewallen and Wang [1998] developed an exact analytical solution for 1-D consolidation of adouble-porosity column of rock. In that solution, the histories of two distinct pore pressures,as wellas displacement of the column's top surface, were shown to be functions of the contrast between mechanical and flow properties of the primary and secondary porosities. More recently,the 2-D counterparts to this problem—known as Mandel-type problems of poroelasticity—have been successfully upgraded to the case of dual-porosity and dual-permeability materials [Nguyen and Abousleiman,2010].Furtheranalytical solutions in this context arise from theirapplicationsintheoiland gasindustry,suchas the welbore stability[Zhang etal.,20o3;Abousleiman and Nguyen,2005] and compaction [Nguyen and Abousleiman,2009] of naturaly fractured reservoirs,or in biomechanics, including the dual-porosity poroelasticanalyses of bone [Smit et al.,2002],brain[Wirthand Sobey,2oo9],and intervertebral disc tissues [Huyghe etal.,2003].

Despiteits elegant and natural approach in extending Biot's theory to acount fortwo distinct pore structures in an elastic medium,the dual-porosity/dual-permeability model may fallshort in capturing some crucial aspects offlow and/or deformation in many fluid saturated porous materials.For this reason, generalizations to this model has been the subject of studies which consider either flow[Gwo etal.,1995] or coupled flowdeformation processes [Bai et al.,1993] in naturally fractured rocks.Shale is also known to comprise multiple-fracture networks ranging from the nanoscale to the sequence/systems tract scale in size and hydraulic conductivity[Slatt and Abousleiman,2011].Their very intricate pore structure has recently been the focus of investigations which aim to model the storage capacity and productivity [Dehghanpour and Shirdel, 2011; Deng et al.,2011] or geomechanics [Abousleiman et al.,2010]of oil- or gas-bearing shale formations.

Biomaterials such as bone and brain tissues are also well known for their multiple pore structure.For example, Hierarchial poroelasticity[Cowinetal,20o9]and Rusiandollporoelasticity[GailaniandCowin,2011]are the terms attributed to the specific models which addressthe interstitialfluid exchange between vascular and lacunar-canalicular porosities of the bone tissue.Likewise,the multiple-network poroelasticity[Tully and Ventikos,2011] describes complexitiesassociated with the brain tissue'smechanical deformation,arising from fluid transport and exchange through four porosity networks.These networks comprise the extracelllar space (matrix),as wellas three distinct porosities related to arteries,veins,and capilary blood vessels within the parenchyma.

This paper extends the existing theoriesof single-anddual-porosity,fluid-saturated,homogenous,isotropic, and elastic materials toaccount for higher levels of pore structure complexity,namely,the multiple-porosity and multiple-permeability poroelasticity.A number of conceptual models comprising groups of springs and fluid containers are presented in section 2.These models ilustrate the poroelastic response of materials with a different numbers of coexisting porosity networks to external loading,through which a generalized constitutive stres-strain relationship for multiple poroelastic media is developed. Complete mechanical characterization of multiple-porosity mixtures is stillan open problem in mechanics of porous media [Berryman,20o2].This problem isaddressed foraporoelastic mixture in Appendix A.For this purpose,a series of thought experiments are designated in order to find the mixture's mechanical properties as functions of similar intrinsic properties of its porous constituents.

Multiple-porosityand multiple-permeability theory,together with the effect of interporosity exchanges of pore fluid,are capable of handling related applied problems of rock mechanics.In order to show this capability, Mandel's problems are revisited in section 3.Owing to its generality and broad applications,a closed form analytical solution to this problem for the case of multiple-porosity and multiple-permeability poroelasticity is developed.Section 4 presents a numerical example of this solution for a triple-porosity sample of shale undergoing some confining stress with the boundary conditions adapted to Mandel's problem. Results are discussed and compared for the three cases of single-,double-and triple-porosity poroelastic models.

# 2. Poroelasticity

# 2.1. Single Porosity

Biot's theoryof poroelasticity[Biot,1941,1955]incorporates the transient effects of pore fuid pressure in the analysis of the elastic skeleton's deformation. With constituents'compresibility taken into account, this theory manifests the volumetric deformations of solid and fluid phases through three independent material properties.The corresponding constitutive relations can be writen in terms of drained matrix bulk modulus $K ,$ Skempton's pore pressure coefficient $B$ [Skempton,1954],and Biot-Willis coefficient $\alpha$ [Biot and Willis,1957] as follows [Berryman,2002]:
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
\varepsilon \\
\zeta
\end{pmatrix}
=
\frac{1}{K}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & \alpha \\
\alpha & \alpha/B
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
\sigma \\
p
\end{pmatrix}
$$
where $\sigma$ and $p$ stand for the average normal stress (tensile positive) and excess pore pressure and $\varepsilon$ and $\zeta$ denote the volumetric strain (Positive in expansion)and increment of fluid content (positive when fluid added to porous space), respectively.

An abstract presentation of poroelastic response to external stressis shown in Figure 1 in a method similar to schematics of Terzaghi's soil consolidation experiment [Wang,20o0].In this setup,the fluid-filled container bounded by a piston and needle valve on top and together with a spring of stiffness $k _ { s }$ undergo a sudden compressive load F.The excess pore pressure instantaneously rises to $p ( 0 ^ { + } ) ,$ the occurrence of which is known as Skempton's effect [Skempton,1954] in poromechanics literature. Until its complete dissipation,the resisting force produced by this pressure gradually transfers to the solid skeleton.The rate of pore pressure dissipation in this process depends on both the hydraulic conductivity $\kappa$ and effective stiffness of the porous mixture.

The quasi-static state of mechanical equilibrium in this setup defines its constitutive relation.This relation can be formulated as follows:

$$
F = k_s \Delta l(t) + A p(t)
$$

where Ais the container's cross-section area.For a more concrete case which addresses deformations of porous elastic materials,the corresponding constitutive relation in three dimensions is written as follows [Rice and Cleary, 1976]:

$$
\sigma _ { i j } = \left( 2 G \varepsilon _ { i j } + \lambda \varepsilon \delta _ { i j } \right) - \alpha p \delta _ { i j }
$$

where $G , \lambda$ are Lamé's constants.The minus sign for $a p$ implies a tensile-positive convention for the stresses.The use of Kronecker delta function $\delta _ { i j }$ in equation (3) is due to the fact that the pore fluid pressure does not affect the solid skeleton's shear deformations.Equations (1) and (3) complete the required constitutive relations for the general three-dimensional deformation of a homogenous,isotropic,and linearly poroelastic medium.

$$

# 2.3. Multiple Porosity and Multiple Permeability

Following Berryman's postulate for characterizing dual-porosity poroelastic materials,a generalized form of equation (4) can be written as follows:

$$
 \begin{pmatrix} \varepsilon \\ \zeta_1 \\ \vdots \\ \zeta_N \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} & \dots & a_{1(N+1)} \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ a_{(N+1)1} & \dots & a_{(N+1)(N+1)} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \sigma \\ p_1 \\ \vdots \\ p_N \end{pmatrix} $$

# 2.4. Field Equations Featuring Interporosity Exchange Effects

The governing equations are herein outlined in a mixed formulation,that is,the volumetric strain ε and the excess pore pressure $p$ are considered as the independent variables in the equilibrium and diffusion equations.The continuity of the fluid phase of porous matrix can be formulated through the following equation:

$$
\frac { \partial \zeta _ { i } } { \partial t } + \nabla . \vec { q } _ { i } = \sum _ { j = 1 } ^ { N } \Gamma _ { i j } \left( p _ { j } - p _ { i } \right) \quad i = 1 , . . . , N
$$

where the terms on the right-hand sides of equation (11) correspond to the sum of allinterporosity flows from porosity networkjtoi. Thesefluxes are assumed to be proportional to their pore pressure contrast with a not necessarily symmetric coeficients matrix. However, without loss of generality,the assumption of

symmetry $\Gamma _ { i j } = \Gamma _ { j i }$ is implied through the derivations and solutions of this study. Equation (11),together with Darcy's law for each porosity network $\vec { q } _ { i } = - \kappa _ { i } \nabla p _ { i }$ $( i = 1 , . . . , N )$ gives the following:

$$
\frac { \partial \zeta _ { i } } { \partial t } - \overline { { \kappa } } \nabla ^ { 2 } p _ { i } = \sum _ { j = 1 } ^ { N } \Gamma _ { i j } \left( p _ { j } - p _ { i } \right) \quad i = 1 , . . . , N

$$
\sum _ { j = 1 } ^ { N } \frac { \partial {\sigma _ { i j }} } {\partial{x _ { j }} } = F _ { i } , \quad i , j = 1 , . . . , 3

# 3.Mandel's Problem

Thetwo-dimensional Mandel's problem [Mandel,1953] investigatesconsolidationof aporous rectangular strip of porous rock sealed and loaded from the top and botom, while the fluid can freely drain from the sides. Likewise,a porous fluid-saturated sphere loaded on its permeable surface,from which the pore fluid can freely drain bya constant uniform pressure,is known as Cryer's problem [Cryer,1963].It has been shown throughanalytical solutions[Mandel,1953;Abousleiman etal.,1996]and laboratory experiments [Gibson et al.,1963] that the pore fluid pressure in the interior of the sample jumps byan amount when the surface traction is applied and then continues to increase for some time before decaying. Being associated with poroelastic coupling,this phenomenon is calld the Mandel-Cryer effect in poromechanics literature [Wang,2000].

Theindustry practice of Mandel-type problems is quite common. Uniaxial and triaxial testing of porous rock specimens [Dickey et al.,1968; Abousleiman and Cui,1998] or investigations of sudden stress relief of a long core removed from subsurface welbore [Wang,20o0] are examples of its laboratory applications. Further,it has been shown thatifits boundary conditions are properly selected,a Mandel-type problem with hollow axisymmetric geometry can be used to analyze the compaction of producing reservoirs [Nguyen and Abousleiman,2009]. Mandel's problem has also been the focus of biomechanical studies such as unconfined compression ofarticularcartilage[Armstrong etal.,1984]and bone[Cowinand Mehrabadi,1992].

Analytical solutions to Mandel-type problems with extension of Biot's theory to dual-porosity/dualpermeability Mandel-type problems have recently been developed [Nguyen and Abousleiman,2010].Using similar methodology and in line with formulations of section 2,solutions to the general case of multipleporosity/multiple-permeability Mandel's problem is developed here.Figure 4 displays the geometry of this problem,along with boundary conditions adapted to this problem.

# 5. Conclusions

This study offered an extension to the existing theories of single-and dual-porosity,fluid-saturated,and elastic media to account for higher levels of pore structure complexity, namely,the multiple-porosity and multiple-permeability poroelasticity.Ilustrative and elaborate presentations of the conceptual models provided in this paper attempted to explore the fundamental concepts underlying a multiple-poroelastic responseto externaltractionsand further to develop their generalized constitutive relations.In particular,the Appendix A presents a closure solution to the problem of material property coeficients of a multiple-porosity mixture, whose constituents follow Biot's formulations of stress-strain relation.

A complete analytical solution to Mandel's problem of multiple poroelasticity is developed. Results indicate that the problem's time scales,and consequently,the associated coupled phenomenon,i.e., Mandel-Cryer effect, would proliferate when the number of distinct porosity networks in the analytical model is increased. Neglecting or oversimplifying the efects of distinguishable pore structures within the specimen's matrix might produce erors which often tend to oversimplify the trends of variation of either flow or deformation aspects of its intricate response to application of stress or pore pressure disturbances.

Addition of the volume fraction $v _ { j }$ in equation (A1) accounts for definition of $\zeta _ { j }$ in terms of the unit volume of the ith porosity network.

# A2.Multiple-Porosity Poroelastic Mixture

The procedure for finding poroelastic coeficients of a multiple-porosity material follows and extends the formulations of previous section.Similartoillustrations ofFigure 7a,Figure 8 presents the setup fora groupof thought experiments on the multiple-porosity model at microscopic level, where $N$ imaginary vents $W _ { 1 } , \dots W _ { N }$ are allowing for drained or undrained condition of confinement for individual porosity networks.

Again,we consider the most general case in which the controlling parameters $\sigma , p _ { 1 } , . . . , p _ { 2 }$ can be adjusted independently.The constitutive relations for individual components of the composite for such experiment can be written as

$$

$$

\sum _ { k = 1 } ^ { n } v _ { k } \epsilon _ { k } = \epsilon

The elements of the coeficient matrix in equation (A25) may be interpreted through corespondence with single-porosity poroelastic properties as follows: $a _ { 1 1 }$ is the mixture's effective compressibility,which is derived as the weighted average of the components'compressbilities.This result is consistent with conventional averaging methods for the compressbility of mixtures.The reminder elements in first row of coeficient matrix represent the generalized Biot-Wilis coefcients.It should be noted that none of these elements represent the effective Biot-Wills coefficient of the whole system.In order to find such property, we examine the undrained thought experiment,as shown in Figure 7b.We can define the overall undrained bulk modulus of a multiporosity system as

$$
\overline { { { \alpha } } } = \biggl ( \frac { 1 - \overline { { { K } } } / \overline { { { K } } } _ { u } } { B } \biggr ) = - \sum _ { j = 2 } ^ { n + 1 } a _ { 1 j }
